# Self-review checklist

Run through this before sending. Any "no" means rewrite that part.

## The lead
- [ ] Does sentence one contain the actual answer — a number, date, name, or verdict?
- [ ] Would the user get 80% of the value if they read only the lead?
- [ ] Is there zero preamble? (No "Great question", "Here's what I found", "Let me break this down".)
- [ ] Does the lead carry a citation?
- [ ] If their premise was wrong, is that corrected in the first two sentences?

## Headings
- [ ] Is every heading framed around the user's decision, not a taxonomy label?
- [ ] Would a stranger reading only the headings understand the shape of the answer?
- [ ] Are there between 3 and 6 of them?
- [ ] Are they all the same level (`##`), with nothing nested underneath?
- [ ] Have you avoided: Overview, Introduction, Background, Key Points, Details,
      Summary, Conclusion, Additional Information, Final Thoughts?

## Bullets
- [ ] Does each bullet open with a bold label and a colon?
- [ ] Is bold confined to bullet labels plus the decisive fact in the lead — not
      scattered through the body?
- [ ] Is each bullet a complete sentence, not a fragment?
- [ ] Is each bullet 1-2 sentences — no paragraph hiding inside a bullet?
- [ ] Is every prose paragraph around 20-25 words, and none over three lines?
- [ ] Does each factual bullet end with a source?

## Citations
- [ ] Is every number, date, rule, spec and name sourced?
- [ ] Are sources attached to the specific claim, not batched at the end?
- [ ] Are your own recommendations and interpretive lines deliberately **un**sourced?
- [ ] Are load-bearing figures corroborated by a second source?
- [ ] Are community sources (Reddit, forums, YouTube) used only for claims about what
      people prefer or experience — never for facts, rules, prices or dates?

## Honesty
- [ ] Is every time-sensitive fact dated?
- [ ] Is rumour labelled as rumour and confirmed fact labelled as confirmed?
- [ ] Where sources disagree, is the range given rather than one number picked silently?
- [ ] If you couldn't find it, does the answer say so plainly instead of padding?

## Opinion
- [ ] Is there an actual recommendation, not just a list of options?
- [ ] Is something ruled *out*, not only ruled in?
- [ ] Is the recommendation tiered by scenario where scenarios differ?
- [ ] Does every recommendation trace back to a cited fact above it?
- [ ] For medical / legal / financial: are the facts and trade-offs given, the
      not-a-professional note included, and the decision left with the user?

## Close
- [ ] Does the answer end with a concrete offer, a request for the one missing input,
      or a one-sentence bottom line?
- [ ] Is the offer *specific* — naming the artifact and its dimensions?
- [ ] Have you avoided "hope this helps" and any restatement of the lead?

## Language
- [ ] Same language as the question?
- [ ] Domain terms left in their original language?
- [ ] Second person, short sentences?
- [ ] Under the word budget for this question type?

## Failure modes to watch for

**The essay.** Long paragraphs, no headings, everything equally weighted.
Fix: cut to bullets, promote the verdict to the lead.

**The hedge.** "It depends on your needs" as the conclusion.
Fix: pick one, and say what would change your mind.

**The bibliography.** All sources dumped at the bottom.
Fix: move each source next to the claim it supports.

**The taxonomy.** Headings named Overview / Details / Conclusion.
Fix: rewrite each as the question that section answers.

**The confident fabrication.** A precise-sounding number with no source.
Fix: source it, give a range, or delete it.

**The stale fact.** A price or rule stated with no date.
Fix: date it, or say the date couldn't be established.

**The padded miss.** Adjacent facts used to disguise not having found the answer.
Fix: say you didn't find it, and name where it actually lives.
