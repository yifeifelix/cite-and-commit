**Both of them are out of date — 2028 is gone, but the requirement itself very much isn't.** The live deadline is **EPC C by 1 October 2030** for all privately rented homes in England and Wales, confirmed in the government's consultation response published 21 January 2026. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/outcome/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-government-response-html), [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030))

## Where the 2028 date came from

- **The original rule:** the pre-2023 plan was EPC C for new tenancies by 2025 and all tenancies by 2028. Rishi Sunak scrapped it in September 2023. ([Simply Business](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/landlord-regulation/new-energy-efficiency-rules-for-rental-properties/))
- **The 2028 revival:** the February 2025 consultation proposed reintroducing 2028, but only for *new* tenancies, with 2030 for existing ones. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-consultation-document-html))
- **What was actually decided:** the government dropped the split. There is now a single date, 1 October 2030, with no earlier trigger for new lettings. ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030))

## The terms that matter to your numbers

- **Cost cap:** £10,000 per property, up from £3,500 today. If you spend that and still miss C, you register a cost-cap exemption. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/outcome/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-government-response-html))
- **Low-value properties:** if the property is worth under £100,000, you only have to spend 10% of its value. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/outcome/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-government-response-html))
- **Penalty:** up to £30,000 per non-compliant property, up from £5,000. ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030))
- **Grandparenting:** get an EPC scoring C or better under the *current* methodology before 1 October 2029 and you're treated as compliant until that certificate expires. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/outcome/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-government-response-html))

## Caveat: it isn't law yet

This is confirmed policy, not enacted regulation. The secondary legislation amending MEES is expected to be laid and come into force in 2027, and that's when the exemption detail becomes legally fixed. ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030)) EPCs themselves are also being rebuilt on new fabric and heating metrics, with new-style certificates from October 2026. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update/outcome/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-government-response-html))

Practically: if your property is currently a D and close to C, get an EPC under the old methodology before October 2029 and use the grandparenting route — it's much cheaper than retrofitting to the new dual-metric standard. If it's an E or F, start planning spend now rather than competing for trades in 2029.
